## Walk the prototype

Open [the local visit](http://127.0.0.1:3077/dev/travellers-rest). To restart it, run `npm run preview:library` at the repository root and use that address. The route uses the existing loopback development guard. It reads its study materials from the checkout and can be explored independently of the portfolio database.

1. At **The approach**, compare the survey photograph with **Explore in 3D**. Rotate the house to see the rear wing. Click the south door, or select **Enter the house**.
2. Follow the first-floor plan from **Entry** to **Library**. In the southwest room, compare the Cycles study, the survey photograph and the interactive model. Select the writing table in 3D or use **Open the house book**.
3. Turn the four leaves at your own pace. The final leaf opens **The family cabinet**. The book identifies its text as a present-day visitor’s companion.
4. In the cabinet, open **Mission Control** to switch between a project recording and a case-study still. Choose **The Royal Palm reel** to enter a separate contemporary screening space and press Play. Closing either exhibit returns to the cabinet. **Return to the marked leaf** takes you back to the final page. The historic-house route requests no match media.
5. Select **Judge John Overton** in the library to examine the Earl portrait. **Collection** opens the portraits, manuscripts, architectural photographs and museum records at any stop. The four leaves each open a related item. **Sources** covers the house evidence. Try Back, a keyboard-only visit and a narrow screen.

## What the sources establish

| Subject | Evidence and design consequence |
| --- | --- |
| The family connection | John, the portfolio owner, identifies Judge John Overton as a relative. Use that wording. A precise genealogical relationship would require family documentation. |
| The judge | John Overton (1766–1833) practiced law in Nashville and served on Tennessee’s early high courts. The state archive describes an Overton letter about his part in Jackson’s 1824 nomination and an 1827 confidential letter to Willie Blount about Jackson’s prospects. His friendship and campaign work belong together in the house book. [State archive finding aid, scope and biographical note](https://tsla.tnsosfiles.com/history/manuscripts/findingaids/MURDOCK_COLLECTION_OF_JOHN_OVERTON_PAPERS_1780-1797-1820-1908.pdf) · [Tennessee Historical Society biography](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/john-overton/) |
| The house and date | The first house was a two-story, four-room Federal timber building completed in 1799. The five-bay south front and L-shaped footprint reflect enlargement and restoration. HABS gives 1812 for the frame addition, circa 1821–29 for the brick ell and 1887 for the rear addition. The Tennessee Encyclopedia gives 1808 for the first addition. The prototype interprets the **restored house surveyed in 1970**, including the later rear mass. Use “begun in 1799” for the building shown. [HABS TN-14, printed pp. 1–3 and 6–9](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/tn/tn0000/tn0036/data/tn0036data.pdf) · [Tennessee Encyclopedia entry](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/travellers-rest/) |
| The library | The HABS first-floor plan on printed p. 8 explicitly labels the southwest room “Library.” The accompanying photograph looks west toward its fireplace. The survey records ash flooring, plaster, chair rails, sash patterns and paneled doors. The south elevation on printed p. 9 establishes the central porch, five bays and end chimneys. The room’s desk, case arrangement, rug, book spines and color palette in this study are interpretive. [Survey plan and elevation](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/tn/tn0000/tn0036/data/tn0036data.pdf) · [South front](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/tn0036.photos.152680p/) · [Library interior](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/tn0036.photos.152686p/) |
| The books | The Tennessee Historical Society puts the law library at about 160 volumes in 1808. The museum identifies the two volumes of the Tennessee Reports, Pope’s Works and a pewter inkwell in its collection. These are the references for the table. Our writing and page layouts are contemporary. [Biography](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/john-overton/) · [Museum collection account](https://www.historictravellersrest.org/history/nashville-from-frontier-to-home-of-the-u-s-president) |
| The people who lived here | A Mississippian community occupied the site centuries before Overton. Enslaved people helped build the home and worked on the plantation as skilled artisans, agricultural workers and household workers. Keep both subjects in the guided visit. Use the museum’s documentation project to substantiate any named individual or personal account. [First Residents](https://www.historictravellersrest.org/history/the-first-residents-of-historic-travellers-rest) · [A Past Uncovered](https://www.historictravellersrest.org/history/a-past-uncovered) |
