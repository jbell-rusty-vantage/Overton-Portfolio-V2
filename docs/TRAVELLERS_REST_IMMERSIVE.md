# Travellers Rest · the continuous 3D library

**Status, end of November 12, 2026.** These instructions were carried out as a private draft. What was built, what was measured and what remains is in [the continuous library review](TRAVELLERS_REST_CONTINUOUS.md). The text below is the brief as given.

**Owner direction, November 12, 2026.** The current design has the right atmosphere and material, but the experience feels fragmented. The next session should make **Enter the library** open a continuous, inhabitable 3D world. Judge John Overton speaks from his portrait and acts as the custodian. Project books leave their shelves and open into readable portfolio pages. Actual letters and other historical objects occupy the room and can be brought close for inspection. The contemporary portfolio has equal importance to the historical interpretation.

**The intended experience.** A visitor crosses the threshold and sees the portrait, shelves, writing table and family cabinet together. Overton notices the visitor and offers a short introduction. The visitor can ask to see John’s work, choose a project spine, watch that book slide out and open at the reading position, turn through the project, then return it to its own shelf. A letter on the desk can be lifted and enlarged, with its manuscript pages, transcript and archival record. Throughout these actions the surrounding room, lighting and location remain perceptible. The custodian can point the visitor to another object, resume a marked page or introduce the contemporary cabinet.

**Build in this order.**

1. **Make entry and navigation continuous.** Give the entrance a clear **Enter the library** action that loads the appropriate model tier, takes the visitor through the doorway and arrives in the southwest room. A direct portfolio action should arrive at the project shelves in the same world. Use one persistent canvas, camera controller and scene root. Represent location, selected volume or artifact, reading position, portrait cue and audio preference as explicit state. A small reducer or an equally clear state model is sufficient.

   Book selection, source inspection and cabinet opening should change the room’s state and camera framing. The directory should navigate to physical destinations. Keep the entrance, library door, portrait, desk and cabinet in their documented spatial relationship. Add controlled movement between these destinations, drag-to-look, keyboard movement and touch equivalents, with sensible boundaries around furniture and walls. Give visitors skip-travel and return controls.

   Preserve the pre-inspection camera pose, selected object and focus target. Browser Back, deep links, reload and closing an object should resolve to a coherent room state. Rapid clicks, a route change during travel or returning a book midway through its motion must settle cleanly. Use one active inspection or reading object at a time. Keep the text catalog and accessible full reader available from the same projected content.

2. **Give the framed portrait a voice and a role.** Begin with the actual Earl likeness in the frame. At the visitor’s invitation, move through a brief recognition, natural gaze shift and speaking performance. Keep the sitter’s facial proportions, hairline, cravat, coat and monochrome source appearance. Animate his mouth to the spoken words and let his gaze or gesture lead to the chosen shelf or document. Finish in a believable resting pose within that frame.

   Deliver authored, voiced cues for greeting, choosing the historical collection or John’s projects, introducing a selected object, returning to a bookmark and handing the visit to the contemporary cabinet. Example visitor choices are **Show me John’s projects**, **Show me the letters**, **Tell me about this room** and **Let me explore**. Resolve choices through the same navigation and selection actions used by the scene. The visible cue should acknowledge what the visitor actually selected.

   Inspect the available voice and animation tools, then choose a reproducible pipeline. A registered video performance on the portrait surface or a facial rig can both support the intended effect. Whichever is used must have synchronized voice, mouth motion, captions and start/pause/resume behavior. Use playback time as the clock, and handle buffering, interruption, hidden tabs and replay. The current recognition clip and browser voice are useful prototypes for testing timing and controls.

   Label newly written narration as an interpretation. Attribute historical quotations to their exact sources. Keep the guide’s portfolio introduction grounded in the owner’s supplied project descriptions. Deliver clear sound-on, mute, pause, replay, captions and silent-reading controls. Reduce motion to the still portrait with the same useful choices. Speech should follow an explicit sound choice and pause when the visitor leaves or starts another media item.

3. **Put the real projects into physical books.** Load the cabinet’s actual projected volumes and pages through the existing collection layer. The last verified private inventory contained fourteen project books. Reconcile the current inventory and owner edits at session start. Give every available project a readable spine, front cover, stable identity and a physical slot. Use the existing shelf names and project data to organize the family cabinet within the room.

   On hover, keyboard focus or touch selection, reveal the title and a restrained invitation to read. Selecting the binding should pull it clear of the shelf, rotate it toward the visitor and open it on the desk or at a comfortable reading position. Animate cover hinges, page thickness, a curved turning leaf and its shadow. Closing reverses the motion and restores the book to its exact slot. Empty slots and re-selection must behave consistently while a volume is out.

   The opened volume must display the project’s actual authored/generated content, including prose, architecture images, code, metrics, links and demo media where present. Reuse `Volume`, `Leaf`, `resolvePages` and the existing block policy. Measure text layout and paginate long sections so content remains readable on both desktop and phone. Keep selectable semantic text and working links aligned with the open pages. A DOM reading surface can be registered to the book while the page geometry handles the turn. Avoid rendering readable body text solely into the current small canvas texture.

   Track a stable volume ID, leaf key and any additional split-page position. Return to that marked place after inspecting a source, visiting another project or reloading. A search or directory result should bring the visitor to that project’s binding and open the same book. Historical law volumes and the contemporary project cabinet should be visibly identifiable in their respective parts of the room.

4. **Make the historical collection tangible.** Place documents where the visitor can discover them while exploring. Use a low-cost room texture at a distance, then fetch the full inspection image on selection. Let the selected document lift, turn toward the camera and settle into a readable position. Provide pan, zoom, fit, page sequence, transcript and a clear **Return to desk** action. Relate transcript passages to the actual manuscript page when a reliable transcription exists. Show archival metadata and source links alongside the inspection controls.

   Begin with the already cataloged **John Overton to Andrew Jackson, November 5, 1832**, Library of Congress item `maj012882`. The catalog describes three pages, while the current checkout has the first-image derivative. Retrieve and inspect the complete digitized sequence, including any cover or docket images, and record the sequence explicitly. The Library of Congress identifies its digital scans of the Andrew Jackson papers as public domain.
