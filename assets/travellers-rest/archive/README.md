# The collection inside Travellers Rest

For the expanded Judge John Overton history through emancipation, see the [exhibit brief](../../../docs/OVERTON_HISTORY.md) and the [new research images](history-research/README.md). The new images are study files; the inventory below describes the collection already prepared for the estate.

**Judge John Overton is the custodian.** The still that comes alive is the [photographic copy of Earl’s portrait](originals/judge-overton.jpg). It shows the Tennessee judge in three-quarter view, with a white cravat, his law reports and two quills. The original oil painting belongs to Travellers Rest. The museum dates it to 1817 and identifies Earl’s logbook entry for Overton commissioning portraits of himself and Jackson together. The digital photograph is monochrome and measures 2,250 × 2,820 pixels. [Tennessee Virtual Archive photograph](https://teva.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p15138coll33/id/285/) · [Museum object record](https://hub.catalogit.app/nscda-tn/folder/entry/judge-john-overton)

## Direct the portrait performance

1. Hang the still at reading height near the west mantel where it can be approached from the hall. That placement is a design choice. The HABS library photograph establishes the room and fireplace. It does not identify the sitter of the small framed image in the room. [Survey photograph](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/tn0036.photos.152686p/)
2. Let the visitor select **Meet Judge Overton**. Start on the unaltered archival image. Ease from Earl’s resting pose into a shift of the eyes toward the visitor, then a slight turn of the head. Hold the likeness through that first recognition. Keep the dark coat, light cravat, nose, hairline, shadows and painted background registered with the source. Begin with a monochrome performance. A future color treatment needs a documented color reference for this canvas.
3. Let his gaze return to the two law reports in the picture. A small gesture inside the frame directs attention from the books beside his quills to the matching volumes on the modeled desk. Offer **Open the reports**, **Follow the house book** and **Explore on my own**. Preserve the marked leaf on return. The archived still and its credit remain inspectable even after he awakens.
4. Identify his words as an interpretive performance. For example, the newly written cue “Look beside the two pens. You can open the reports on the table” directs attention to something visibly present. Provide captions and explicit sound controls. Reserve attributed quotations for transcribed primary material with a page reference. The museum captions introduce the first residents and the enslaved household members. The portfolio owner introduces the contemporary cabinet.
5. In Blender, align the neutral likeness to the photograph, isolate the movable features and give the eyes and head a limited range compatible with a single camera view. Export the short performance with named animation clips for the web scene. Keep both the photographic texture and a static accessible rendering. On reduced motion or skipped animation, the same three controls work from the still. Review the opening and closing frames against the source and test an uninterrupted journey from portrait to book, desk and cabinet.

## Place the collection

| Where the visitor finds it | Object and direction |
| --- | --- |
| In the entry hall | Put the [1795 Tennessee map](originals/tennessee-map.jpg) on a map table with the original margins and a zoom control. Use it to orient visitors to a mapmaker’s view of the region. The site’s earlier Native history has its own archaeological record in the house book. [Library of Congress map](https://www.loc.gov/item/99446175/) |
| On the portrait wall | Hang Earl’s [military portrait of Andrew Jackson](originals/andrew-jackson.jpg). Its tentative museum date is 1817. Explain the friendship and the artist’s connection to both sitters. Whether this particular canvas was Overton’s commission is unknown. [Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery, NPG.65.78](https://npg.si.edu/object/npg_NPG.65.78) |
| At the writing table | Lay out the [first page of Overton’s November 5, 1832 letter to Jackson](originals/overton-letter.jpg). Preserve the handwriting and link the complete four-image record so visitors can inspect the other sheets. [Library of Congress manuscript](https://www.loc.gov/item/maj012882/) |
| In the campaign drawer | The [1828 invitation to a Nashville ball](originals/election-invitation.jpg) was printed on satin. Model the frayed edge and folds. The invitation was issued December 10 for a celebration planned for December 23. [Library of Congress printed ephemera](https://www.loc.gov/item/2020772132/) |
| Inside a volume of the Tennessee Reports | Open the [two-page case scan](originals/murfree-leeper.pdf) at *Murfree v. Leeper*, 1 Tenn. 1 (1 Overt. 1). The 1791 decision predates Overton’s judgeship. His connection is as reporter. The scan supplies the printed page. The binding of his own volume remains a modeling decision. [Harvard Law School Library facsimile](https://static.case.law/tenn/1/case-pdfs/0001-01.pdf) |
| In the house book | Give Mary McConnell White May Overton her own linked portrait record. On the household leaf name the brothers William and Franklin, ages twelve and seven when Judge Overton purchased them in 1832. Give each boy his own catalog link. On the First Residents leaf, use the museum’s excavated ceramic bowl with its notched rim and freshwater snail shells. These entries link the institutions’ photographs while reuse of the images is unresolved. [Mary’s portrait](https://hub.catalogit.app/nscda-tn/folder/entry/mary-mcconnell-white-may-overton) · [William](https://travellersrestenslavedpersonsdocumentationproject.omeka.net/items/show/100) · [Franklin](https://travellersrestenslavedpersonsdocumentationproject.omeka.net/items/show/101) · [First residents and archaeological finds](https://www.historictravellersrest.org/history/the-first-residents-of-historic-travellers-rest) |
| In the architectural reference set | Match the entrance camera and stair to [Jack Boucher’s center hall photograph](originals/center-hall.jpg). Use the [TN-14 survey PDF](originals/tn-14-survey.pdf), printed pp. 8–9, for plan and elevation. Use [Carol Highsmith’s 2021 photograph](originals/house-color.jpg) for the relationship of paint, sun and shadow. The 2021 doorway differs visibly from the survey portico. The restored house in the survey is the chosen geometry. [Center hall photograph](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/tn0036.photos.152684p/) · [HABS survey](https://www.loc.gov/item/tn0036/) · [Highsmith photograph](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021756795/) |

## Use the files

Open [the playable collection](http://127.0.0.1:3077/dev/travellers-rest#library) and select **Judge John Overton**. The selector also opens the photographs, documents and museum records.

- [catalog.json](catalog.json) gives stable object IDs, source and rights links, credit lines, object captions and proposed placements. The three museum-only entries carry a use note. The measured south elevation is a builder reference in the held PDF.
- [originals](originals/) contains the nine downloaded source files. [files.json](files.json) records the hash, byte size and dimensions or page count of each held file and its delivery image when applicable.
- [Web plates](../../../public/travellers-rest/archive/) contains eight images for the visitor, with longest edges up to 1,600 pixels. To regenerate these from the held files, run `python assets/travellers-rest/archive/prepare.py` from the repository root in an environment with Pillow and PyMuPDF.
- For the draft seed, use each catalog ID to relate its display media, historical credit and scene placement. Use the hash in each delivery record for blob reuse. Store credits and record links with the placement so they survive a texture or scene-tier change. Link museum-only records directly and keep the measured elevation PDF in the builder set. Track any animated or reconstructed derivative separately from the archival original.

## Correspondence held for the continuous library

Four items from the Library of Congress Andrew Jackson Papers are held complete, every digitized image included, with transcriptions checked against the manuscripts. All four are from John Overton to Andrew Jackson. [letters.json](letters.json) records each image, its hash, what it shows, the transcription with page positions, the edition used and what remains uncertain.

| Item | What the images are | Text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [November 5, 1832](https://www.loc.gov/item/maj012882/), on the writing table | Four images: two written pages, the blank inner side of the second leaf, and its outer side with a filing endorsement. The catalog's "3 pages" is read here as the three inscribed sides; that reading is an inference. | Checked line by line against *The Papers of Andrew Jackson*, vol. X (2016), p. 553. One closing word differs from the edition and is marked `[is?]`. |
| [June 2, 1831](https://www.loc.gov/item/maj012284/), correspondence drawer | Four images: Overton's signed statement on the Seminole campaign, a blank side, and the address panel with Jackson's endorsement. | Checked against *Papers*, vol. IX, p. 282. The date comes from the endorsement; the place is inferred. |
| [August 25, 1827](https://www.loc.gov/item/maj011135/), correspondence drawer | Four images: three written pages and the address panel. | No published full text was found. The opening, the close and the address panel were transcribed from the images; page 2 is not transcribed. The postmark reads "Augt 23rd" against the cataloged 25th. |
| [January 19, 1812](https://www.loc.gov/item/maj001399/), correspondence drawer | Three images: the letter, its close with a signed receipt, and the address panel. | Transcribed from the images; the edition only calendars it. |

No letter from Jackson to Overton could be confirmed from a Library of Congress record in this pass. Candidates that turned out to be a receipt, a clerk's copy, or the hand of a different Overton were left out.

Inspection derivatives for the map, the invitation, Earl's Jackson and both pages of *Murfree v. Leeper* are produced by [prepare-world.py](prepare-world.py) and recorded in [world-files.json](world-files.json). Each object has a close-inspection WebP up to 2,400 pixels on its long edge and a 640-pixel texture for the room. The three museum records stay as source-linked cards in a tray on the writing table.
