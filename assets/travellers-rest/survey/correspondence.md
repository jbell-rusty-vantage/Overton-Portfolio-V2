# Jackson to Overton: candidate letters

Survey date: 2026-11-12. Scope: letters written by Andrew Jackson to Judge John Overton (1766-1833) that have digitised manuscript images online and clear reuse terms. Nothing was downloaded into the project; images were looked at from a temporary folder outside it.

## Summary

- **Two letters qualify**, both from 1829, both confirmed from the holding institution's own record and by looking at the images:
  1. **8 June 1829**, Tennessee State Library and Archives (TSLA). The letter Overton actually received: in Jackson's hand, signed, franked and postmarked, addressed to "The Honble John Overton near Nashville Tennessee". Rights: "No copyright - United States". Add this one first.
  2. **31 December 1829**, New York Public Library (NYPL). A retained copy in William B. Lewis's hand, signed and endorsed by Jackson. It is not the sheet that went to Overton. NYPL treats it as public domain in the US and serves full-size images.
- **The Library of Congress holds no Jackson-to-Overton letter.** The one item titled that way (maj000575, 27 May 1802) is a debt memorandum in Overton's hand, the same kind of document rejected last session.
- **Almost all the other Jackson-to-Overton autograph letters belong to the Tennessee Historical Society** (Claybrooke and Overton Papers; Murdock Collection of John Overton Papers), held at TSLA. The finding aids describe them as microfilmed. No manuscript images of them were found online, so none is a candidate. They are listed below with their printed transcriptions in case images are ordered later.
- No third, fourth or fifth candidate was found. The table has two rows because only two exist online on the evidence gathered.

The hand of the letter, the signature and the frank agree with one another, and the edition classes the letter as an autograph. I did not compare the hand against another Jackson autograph myself.

**Resolution limit:** these are the largest images TeVA serves. The [download PDF](https://teva.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p15138coll33/id/144/download) (16.5 MB) holds the same 150 ppi images. The record says: "All images on TeVA are available to download as lower-resolution JPEG files ... To order a high-resolution digital reproduction of this item, please see these instructions". The pages of the four LoC letters already held are between 1778 and 3357 px wide, most of them over 2200, so this letter would display at roughly half their resolution unless a high-resolution scan is ordered from TSLA for a fee.

**Published transcription:**
- Daniel Feller, Harold D. Moser, Laura-Eve Moss, and Thomas Coens, eds., *The Papers of Andrew Jackson, Volume VII, 1829* (Knoxville: University of Tennessee Press, 2007), pp. 270-272, "To John Overton"; source line "ALS, T (13-0595)". Open access: [volume record](https://trace.tennessee.edu/handle/20.500.14382/48164), [PDF](https://trace.tennessee.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/74d3e800-062c-4d1a-aae7-998d8aea6fc2/content), DOI 10.7290/utp1159. Use this as the base text. Its opening and close were compared with the images and agree.
- TSLA's own typed transcript, linked from the item record: [43090.pdf](https://tsla.tnsosfiles.com/digital/teva/transcripts/43090.pdf). This address returned HTTP 403 to a bare client name and 200 to a browser-style one; the same file also came back from the [storage address](https://s3.amazonaws.com/tsla.tnsosfiles.com/digital/teva/transcripts/43090.pdf). It is page-by-page, which helps with layout, but it has misreadings where the edition is right: "penance of Mexico" for "finance of Mexico", "How unfortunate" for "How infatuated".
- Not in Bassett: the full text of *Correspondence of Andrew Jackson*, vol. 4, was searched for "sandy desert" and "silent walks" without result, and its contents list has no Overton letter for June 1829.

**What it says, and why it suits the library:** Jackson, three months into his presidency, answers two of Overton's letters. He wants the boundary pushed west of the Sabine to regain Texas, has sent William Carroll to press the Cherokee and Creek to remove west of the Mississippi, wants the Bank of the United States replaced by a national bank, defends Major Eaton, and ends that grief and ill health make him doubt he will spend much more time "on the silent walks of the Hermitage". It is the only letter found that Overton himself opened at Travellers Rest, so it answers the four Overton-to-Jackson letters already on display.

**Uncertainties:**
- TSLA's description calls it a "Six-page letter"; the item has eight images (six of text, the address panel, a later envelope).
- The collection finding aid named in the record (archives.tnsos.gov, ark:/55409/288047) could not be read: HTTP 202 with an empty body to curl, 403 to a scraper. How the letter reached the Dickinson Papers is therefore not verified.
- The page content includes Jackson's case for Indian removal. That is a presentation decision for the site, not a rights question.

Preferred citation, from the finding aid: "Andrew Jackson and William B. Lewis correspondence, Manuscripts and Archives Division, The New York Public Library".

**Published transcriptions:**
- *The Papers of Andrew Jackson, Volume VII, 1829* (as above), pp. 655-657, "To John Overton", with Jackson's endorsement and source line "LS copy in William B. Lewis's hand, NN (14-1192)", followed by Lewis's note. Same open-access links as candidate 1.
- John Spencer Bassett, ed., *Correspondence of Andrew Jackson*, vol. 4, 1829-1832 (Washington: Carnegie Institution of Washington, 1929), pp. 108-109, "To John Overton", footnote "Copy. N. Y. Pub. Lib., Ford MSS."; Lewis's note is given in a second footnote. [Internet Archive copy](https://archive.org/details/correspondenceof0004jack), [full text](https://archive.org/download/correspondenceof0004jack/correspondenceof0004jack_djvu.txt). The Internet Archive record dates the set 1926; a second copy on the site gives 1929 for this volume.

**What it says, and why it might suit the library:** Jackson, gravely ill, tells Overton that Van Buren is "well qualified" for the highest office and that he cannot say as much for Calhoun, and asks Overton to write newspaper pieces for dividing the surplus revenue among the states. Lewis's note explains that he asked Jackson to write it to "his old and confidential friend" so it could be used for Van Buren if Jackson died. It shows Overton as the friend Jackson chose to hold his political testament.

**Uncertainties and the main drawback:**
- This sheet was Lewis's, not Overton's. The edition records no recipient's copy; whether the letter Overton received survives is not verified. A display must label it as a retained copy.
- Only five of the eight images are the letter; three are Lewis's note and the docket.

## Why candidate 1 first

It is the only one that was in Overton's hands, it is wholly in Jackson's writing, and its address panel names Overton and his home near Nashville. Its record, rights statement, images and open-access transcription were all confirmed. Its weakness is image size. Candidate 2 has much better images and equally clear terms, but it is a secretary's copy kept in Washington, so it is the weaker fit for a room that reconstructs Overton's own library. If only one is added, add candidate 1 and consider ordering TSLA's high-resolution scan.

## Not found or not confirmed

- Any Jackson-to-Overton draft, retained copy or letterbook copy in the Library of Congress. None is catalogued under that title pattern, and the edition gives no "DLC" source line for any Jackson-to-Overton letter.
- A recipient's copy of the 31 December 1829 letter.
- Any Tennessee Historical Society letter as an online image, on TeVA or elsewhere. The Society's own website could not be reached (connection failed), so its digital presence is not verified.
- The finding aid for the Jacob McGavock Dickinson Papers (blocked, see candidate 1).
- Gilder Lehrman, The Hermitage, and the University of Tennessee special collections: not checked in their own catalogues. Two web searches for Gilder Lehrman returned nothing; the University of Tennessee's digital collections and finding-aid sites returned bot-check pages. Absence there is not established.
- The Papers of Andrew Jackson digital edition (Rotunda) is paywalled and was not consulted.
- *Tennessee Historical Quarterly* 6 (1947), cited by the edition for many of the THi letters (pp. 161-174 across the citations seen): article title and access not verified.
- The Library of Congress site-wide search returned HTTP 503 on three attempts and a plain item page returned 403 near the end of the session; requests to loc.gov were stopped. The collection-level searches above had completed before that.
